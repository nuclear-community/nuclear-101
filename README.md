# 🎓 nuclear-101

**A structured, self-study path through nuclear science and engineering — from the nucleus of an atom to the safety case of a power reactor.**

Maintained by the [Nuclear Science & Engineering Community](https://github.com/nuclear-community). Free forever, built in the open, improved by people like you.

## Who this is for

- **Students** — undergraduates in engineering, physics, or chemistry who want a head start (or a clearer map) of the field
- **Career changers** — engineers and scientists moving into the nuclear sector
- **Enthusiasts** — anyone who wants to understand how nuclear technology *actually* works, beyond headlines
- **Professionals** — refreshing fundamentals outside your specialty

No nuclear background is assumed. You need high-school physics and comfort with basic calculus (derivatives, integrals, exponentials). Everything else is built up along the way.

## The path

Work through the modules in order — each builds on the previous one. Every module is self-contained: objectives up front, an annotated topic outline, curated (free-first) resources, and self-check questions with which you can test yourself before moving on.

A realistic pace is **2–4 weeks per module** studying a few hours per week. Slower is fine — this is a marathon, not a sprint.

## How to study

1. **Read the objectives first.** They tell you what you should be able to *do* after the module.
2. **Skim the topic outline**, then read it properly, following the resources that match your learning style (video lectures, textbooks, interactive tools).
3. **Do the self-check questions** without looking back. If you miss one, revisit that topic before moving on.
4. **Get unstuck in [Discussions](https://github.com/nuclear-community/.github/discussions)** — asking a good question is a contribution, not an imposition.
5. **Spot an error or a gap?** That's the most valuable thing you can do next — see [Contributing](#contributing).
