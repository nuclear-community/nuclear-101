# 06 · Fuel cycle & waste

*From a rock in the ground to a cask in a repository — the full journey of nuclear fuel.*

## Overview

The reactor is one station on a long road. This module follows uranium from mining to enrichment, through the reactor, and out the other side as spent fuel — then confronts the field's hardest socio-technical problem: what to do with the waste, and whether "waste" is even the right word.

## Prerequisites

- [Module 03 — Reactor physics](../03-reactor-physics/) (fuel composition, burnup)
- [Module 05 — Radiation detection & protection](../05-radiation-detection-protection/) (dose context for waste handling)

## Learning objectives

After this module you can:

- Describe the front end: mining, milling, conversion, enrichment, fabrication
- Explain isotopic enrichment and separative work units (SWU) at a conceptual level
- Break down spent fuel composition and explain why it's hot and radioactive
- Compare the open (once-through) and closed (reprocessing) fuel cycles with arguments for each
- Describe waste classes and the geological disposal concept, including real-world progress

## Topics

### 1. Front end — getting fuel ready
- Uranium in the Earth's crust (~2.8 ppm average; ore grades from 0.01% to >10%)
- Mining: open-pit, underground, in-situ leaching (ISL) — and their trade-offs
- Milling to U₃O₈ "yellowcake"
- Conversion to UF₆ (gas at modest temperature — enabling separation)
- **Enrichment**: why ²³⁵U (0.72% natural) must be raised to 3–5% for LWR fuel
  - Gas centrifuges: mass difference in a spinning gas; cascade structure
  - Separative work units (SWU) — enrichment as an industrial commodity
  - Laser enrichment (SILEX/AVLIS) — awareness level
- Fabrication: pellets → rods → assemblies; the QC that keeps cladding trustworthy

### 2. In the reactor — a chemistry refresher
- Burnup changes everything: ²³⁵U depletes, ²³⁸U breeds ²³⁹Pu, fission products accumulate
- Fuel stays in the core 3–5 years across 3–4 cycles (quarter/half core reloads)

### 3. Back end — spent fuel and waste
- **Spent fuel composition** (rough, per tonne after typical LWR burnup): ~93–95% ²³⁸U, ~1% ²³⁵U, ~1% plutonium, ~4% fission products, ~0.1% minor actinides (Np, Am, Cm)
- Why it's dangerous: fission products (Cs-137, Sr-90 dominate the first centuries; ²⁴¹Am and plutonium isotopes the millennia) — heat + gamma + radiotoxicity
- Wet storage (cooling pools, ≥5 years) → dry casks (decades, passive cooling, ready for transport)
- **Reprocessing** (PUREX): separating U and Pu; MOX fuel; recycling economics and proliferation concerns
- Fast reactors and "burning" actinides — the closed-cycle vision
- **Waste classes**: VLLW, LLW (volume-dominated: ~90% of waste, low hazard), ILW, HLW (spent fuel / vitrified reprocessing waste: small volume, ~95% of radioactivity)
- **Geological disposal**: multi-barrier concept (waste form, canister, buffer, host rock); Finland's Onkalo — the world's first operating deep geological repository for spent fuel — and Sweden's approved Forsmark program
- Why "waste for 100,000 years" is both true and misleading — radiotoxicity declines to ore-equivalent over ~10⁵–10⁶ years

### 4. Thorium & the wider cycle
- The thorium cycle (²³²Th → ²³³U): abundance, proliferation arguments, and why it's not used (no free lunch: still needs fissile driver or startup inventory)
- Fuel cycles as policy: once-through (US, Sweden) vs. closed (France, Russia, Japan) — the trade-space

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Yellowcake | U₃O₈ concentrate from milling |
| SWU | Separative work unit — the "currency" of enrichment |
| Burnup | Energy extracted per tonne of fuel (MWd/tU, GWd/tU) |
| MOX | Mixed-oxide fuel (U + recycled Pu) |
| PUREX | The standard reprocessing chemistry (Plutonium-URanium EXtraction) |
| Multi-barrier | Layered defense of geological disposal |

2. Explain why a gas centrifuge separates ²³⁵UF₆ from ²³⁸UF₆.

4. Why does spent fuel need ~5 years of pool cooling before dry storage?
5. Give two arguments for and two against reprocessing.
6. Why do Finland and Sweden lead the world on geological disposal while others stall? (Think: consent-based siting, institutional persistence.)
