# 08 · Applications & society

*Reactor generations, SMRs, medicine, and the public conversation.*

## Overview

The final module zooms out: which reactors exist (and which are coming), what nuclear technology does besides electricity, how it fits into the energy and climate picture, and how to talk about all of it in public — the skill the field most undervalues.

## Prerequisites

- [Modules 01–07](../) — this module assumes the whole path behind you

## Learning objectives

After this module you can:

- Place reactor designs on the Generations I–IV map and explain what distinguishes each
- Explain what an SMR is, what problems it might solve, and what remains unproven
- List major non-power applications (medical, industrial, research) with concrete examples
- Discuss nuclear's role in decarbonization using quantitative anchors (capacity factor, lifecycle emissions)
- Recognize good and bad risk communication about nuclear topics
- Know where to go next: organizations, education paths, and this community

## Topics

### 1. Reactor generations & designs
- The generations map: Gen I (prototypes), Gen II (the 1970s–80s fleet), Gen III/III+ (EPR, AP1000, VVER-1200 — passive & active safety upgrades), Gen IV (six concepts: HTGR/VHTR, SFR, MSR, SCWR, GFR, LFR)
- Why the LWR won the 1950s (submarine heritage) and what that legacy means
- Heavy-water (CANDU), gas-cooled (AGR, HTTR), and fast-reactor lineages (BN-800)
- Passive vs. active safety — the design philosophy shift after TMI/Chernobyl/Fukushima

### 2. Small modular reactors & advanced concepts
- **SMRs**: the argument (factory fabrication, lower upfront capital, grid flexibility, replacement of coal) vs. the open questions (economics at scale, licensing first-of-a-kind, fuel supply chains)
- Microreactors (1–20 MWt class) for remote sites and industrial heat
- High-temperature gas reactors for process heat; molten-salt experiments; fast reactors for actinide management (Module 06 loop)
- Nuclear's non-electric markets: district heating, desalination, hydrogen (high-temperature electrolysis)
- Fusion outlook — where it actually stands (ITER's struggles, private ventures' claims) and a sober timeline

### 3. Nuclear beyond power
- **Medicine**: ⁹⁹Mo/⁹⁹ᵐTc generators (~40 million procedures/year — the workhorse of diagnostic imaging); radiation therapy; theranostics
- **Industrial**: gauges (level, density), neutron radiography, well logging, food irradiation
- **Research reactors**: neutron scattering, isotope production, materials testing (and how most of them use LEU fuel today)
- **Space**: RTGs (Voyager, Curiosity, Perseverance) and fission surface power concepts
- Radiochemistry, activation analysis, and dating techniques (¹⁴C and beyond)

### 4. Nuclear in the energy & climate picture
- Capacity factor: US fleet runs at ~92–93% — the highest of any generation source
- Lifecycle emissions: IPCC median ~12 gCO₂-eq/kWh — comparable to wind
- The system perspective: firm low-carbon power vs. variable renewables; grid studies (MIT's *Future of Nuclear Energy in a Carbon-Constrained World* is the reference)
- Economics: LCOE debates, first-of-a-kind vs. nth-of-a-kind costs, financing (why Vogtle hurt and why some countries still build on schedule)
- Uranium supply and fuel-cycle geopolitics (Module 06 tie-in)

### 5. The public conversation
- Risk perception: dread vs. statistics (deaths per TWh — nuclear's number is among the lowest)
- Waste and accident narratives: how to acknowledge real trade-offs without either dismissing or catastrophizing
- Principles of good science communication: meet people where they are, lead with values not numbers, distinguish "safe" from "acceptably safe"
- Where anti-nuclear and pro-nuclear both get it wrong — a tour of the honest middle

### 6. Getting involved
- Professional orgs: ANS (and student sections), ENS, national societies; Youth in Nuclear-type networks
- Education paths: nuclear/ME/EE/physics/engineering programs; graduate school; vendor and utility career tracks; regulator careers
- Open source & this community: [our projects incubator](https://github.com/nuclear-community/projects) as a place to build a portfolio

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Gen III+ / Gen IV | Current evolutionary designs / next-generation concepts |
| SMR | Small modular reactor (≲300 MWe class, factory-built) |
| Capacity factor | Actual output ÷ maximum possible output |
| LCOE | Levelized cost of electricity |
| ⁹⁹ᵐTc | Technetium-99m — the most-used medical radioisotope |
| RTG | Radioisotope thermoelectric generator (space power) |

## Self-check questions

1. What distinguishes a Gen III+ design from a Gen II design, with one concrete example each?
2. Give the two strongest arguments *for* SMRs and the two biggest open questions about them.
3. Why is ⁹⁹ᵐTc so widely used in medicine?
4. A politician claims nuclear "can't help the climate because it's too slow and too expensive." What honest, quantitative response would you give — and what part of the claim would you concede?
5. Why does capacity factor matter as much as cost per watt when comparing generation sources?
6. Name three non-electricity uses of nuclear technology you'd defend to a skeptic.
